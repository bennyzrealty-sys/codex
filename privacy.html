The Operator's Codex

Privacy

Last updated 12 August 2026

The whole of it: the Codex has no accounts, no server of its own, and no analytics or advertising of any kind. It never asks you for anything, so there is nothing about you for it to hold. What it remembers about your reading is written on your own device and never sent anywhere.

What is stored, and where

The Codex keeps a small amount of state in your browser's localStorage, on this device only. Nothing in it identifies you, and none of it leaves the device:

  • codex.seen.* — which layers you have genuinely sat in, so the atlas tiles and the reading path can tick.
  • codex.read.* — how many times you have opened a card that a sweep marked as newly updated, so the mark can fade after three readings.
  • codex.door.taught — whether you have already been shown the double-click gesture, so you are only shown it once.

Clearing it is entirely in your hands: the button marked clear my progress at the foot of the reading path erases the ticks, and clearing this site's data in your browser settings removes everything above along with the offline copy.

The offline copy

If your browser supports it, the Codex installs a service worker (sw.js) that keeps a copy of the pages, the stylesheet, the script, the typefaces, the diagrams and the live feed in your browser's cache, so the whole thing can be read with no signal. That cache lives on your device. It is written by your browser, read by your browser, and deleted whenever you clear this site's data.

Who else your device talks to

Loading the Codex fetches files from one origin: the one you are reading it on. The typefaces are served from that same origin rather than from Google Fonts, precisely so that no third party is told what you are reading or from where.

There are two exceptions, and both are things you choose:

  • Every feed entry cites its sources as ordinary links. Following one takes you to that publisher's site, which has its own privacy policy and is beyond the Codex's reach.
  • The site is hosted on a static host (GitHub Pages at the time of writing), which — like every web server — sees the request your browser makes for the files, including your IP address. That is the host's ordinary server logging, not something the Codex collects, reads, or has any access to.

Children

The Codex is written to be understood by a beginner of any age. It collects nothing from anyone, so it collects nothing from children either.

Changes

If this ever stops being true — if accounts, sync, or any measurement are added — this page will be rewritten before the change ships, and the date at the top will say when.

Contact

Questions about this page, or about anything the Codex does on your device, can be raised as an issue on the repository the site is built from.
